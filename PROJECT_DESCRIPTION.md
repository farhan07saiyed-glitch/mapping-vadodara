# Mapping Vadodara — Project Description
*Introduction to Alternative Media · End-Semester Project*

**Tagline:** Discover the Stories Behind Every Street.

## 1. What is the platform?
Mapping Vadodara is a hyperlocal alternative-media platform that documents the culture, history, people, public spaces, markets and civic questions of Vadodara, Gujarat. Its core idea is **story-based city mapping**: every place on an interactive map opens into the human stories behind it. The website is the vehicle; the media idea is place-anchored, community-sourced storytelling.

## 2. Why does it need to exist?
Mainstream city coverage focuses on events, officials, crime and well-known landmarks. Everyday streets, informal work, small heritage and neighbourhood problems receive little space, and the residents who know them best have few places to publish. Mapping Vadodara responds to that gap.

## 3. Niche
Hyperlocal, place-based storytelling for one city, organised by neighbourhood and location rather than by news cycle.

## 4. Audience
Residents and students of Vadodara, newcomers to the city, local creators, heritage enthusiasts, and community groups or institutions that want better local documentation.

## 5. What it publishes
Place stories, short interviews (People of Vadodara), hidden-gem entries, culture and heritage notes, reader-submitted stories and clearly signed opinion. Future formats: audio and short video.

## 6. Sources and who creates the content
A student editorial team plus citizen contributors (residents, students, artists, vendors). Public references (e.g. tourism and encyclopaedic sources) are used only as starting points and listed on each story with a "still to verify" list. Interviewees give informed consent and see how they are quoted.

## 7. What makes it different (alternative-media dimensions)
- **Subject:** streets, lanes, markets, small heritage, civic questions.
- **Voice:** local people in their own words.
- **Access and participation:** anyone can contribute; audiences become sources and authors.
- **Ownership:** independent and community-minded.
- **Editorial approach:** slow, sourced, transparent; every item labelled Reference, Opinion or Demonstration.
- **Distribution:** map-led discovery, QR codes at places, WhatsApp groups, social short-form content, local partners.

## 8. How people find it (planned)
QR codes at mapped locations, short videos and photo posts, WhatsApp community groups, college and library notice boards, and partnerships with local organisations.

## 9. Possible sustainability (future plans, not current income)
Ethical local advertising, community donations, cultural partnerships, educational collaborations, local business sponsorships, and grants for independent media. Principle: revenue must not decide what is reported.

## 10. Why it is alternative media
It is organised around a community and a place rather than around news events; it is participatory (citizen contributors), transparent about sourcing, independent of corporate or political ownership, and designed to surface overlooked voices.

## Features implemented
Home with search and featured sections · interactive Leaflet/OpenStreetMap map with category filters, search, popups and links to stories · stories with search, category and content-type filters · story detail pages with sources and "still to verify" · People of Vadodara (fictional demonstration profiles) · Hidden Gems · Culture & Heritage · Share Your Story form (validation, image preview, localStorage simulation) · About (mission, vision, differences from mainstream media, editorial policy, sustainability) · Contact form (demonstration) · responsive layout and mobile navigation.

## Limitations (stated openly)
It is a frontend-only prototype. Forms do not reach any editorial team and nothing is stored on a server. Map pins are approximate and need on-site verification. Several stories and all people profiles are demonstration content. Photographs are placeholders to be replaced with the group's own original photography.

## What we learned (edit to match your group's experience)
- A media idea needs a defined niche, audience and editorial rules before any design.
- Transparency labels (fact / opinion / demonstration) make a small platform more trustworthy.
- Community participation needs consent, privacy and correction processes built in from the start.
